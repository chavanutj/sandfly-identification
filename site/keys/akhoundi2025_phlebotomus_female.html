Mediterranean & Middle East: Phlebotomus (Phlebotomus) females

Females of the same four species; P. papatasi and P. duboscqi are not separated. Read the note on couplet 2.

SourceAkhoundi, M., Maroli, M., Gradoni, L., Izri, A., Dedet, J.-P., Votýpka, J., Guernaoui, S., Benikhlef, R., Eddaikra, N., Bates, P., Sereno, D. & Depaquit, J. (2025) A pictorial identification key for Mediterranean and Middle Eastern Phlebotomus sand flies. Scientific Reports 15: 7492. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-77815-7
RegionMediterranean and Middle East
Sexesfemale

2 Short ascoid never reaching the next articulation: no ⚠ Akhoundi et al. 2025 p. 93
Phlebotomus papatasi☰ ; Phlebotomus duboscqi☰
Note: As printed; the species account of the female of P. papatasi (p. 8) says "Short ascoid not reaching to the junction area with the antennal segment", i.e. the opposite of this lead. Species accounts pp. 6-8 (Figures N°2, N°3).
2′– Short ascoid never reaching the next articulation: yes ⚠ Akhoundi et al. 2025 p. 93
Phlebotomus bergeroti☰
Note: As printed; the species account of the female of P. bergeroti (p. 5) says "Long ascoids reaching or exceeding the subsequent junction antennal segment spaces", i.e. the opposite of this lead. The YES/NO boxes of this couplet may be swapped - check. Species account p. 4-5 (Figures N°1).
