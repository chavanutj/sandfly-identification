Phlebotomus stantoni☰
Update from later literature
P. stantoni s.l.: see P. shadenae (Renaux Torres et al. 2023). P. stantoni: Cambodian ♀♀ generally have four large, very sharp, translucent cibarial teeth (Prasetyo et al. 2026).
3 Male genitalia with 2 small lobes below larger, main lobe on paramere, and long, slender spine on each side of aedeagus; ♀ spermatheca with distal part as broad as basal; ♀ cibarium with several central teeth larger than others ⚠
Phlebotomus argentipes☰
Update from later literature
P. argentipes s.l.: see P. seowpohi (Ding et al. 2025) and the Sri Lanka key (P. argentipes s.s., P. annandalei, P. glaucus). Thai cave Euphlebotomus without a spine beside the aedeagus: P. mascomai (Muller et al. 2007) and P. barguesae (Depaquit et al. 2009); ♀ P. mascomai spermatheca with 20-30 rings, ♀ P. barguesae spermathecae smooth. In Laos, P. argentipes may be confused with P. mascomai (Vongphayloth et al. 2024). P. argentipes in SE Asia: no P. argentipes was found in Laos 2014-2024; argentipes-like specimens were P. seowpohi, and SE Asian records of P. argentipes need re-investigation (Vongphayloth et al. 2026).
3′– Male paramere simple, without ventral lobes; ♀ spermatheca with distal part reduced to slender neck; ♀ cibarium with all teeth of about same size ⚠
Phlebotomus chinensis☰
Update from later literature
Adlerius: two females of an undescribed Adlerius (Phlebotomus sp. 1) from Luangnamtha, Laos - first record of the subgenus in SE Asia outside China; cyt-b distance 14% from Chinese P. chinensis. Adlerius females are mostly indistinguishable; males needed (Vongphayloth et al. 2026).

Sergentomyia brevicaulis☰
Update from later literature
S. brevicaulis: 'S. brevicaulis-like' females from Laos have long pharyngeal teeth as in S. rudnicki; status unresolved (Vongphayloth et al. 2024). S. brevicaulis / S. rudnicki: Lao S. brevicaulis have 47-64 posterior cibarial teeth and one or two rows of 17-26 anterior teeth, and differ from Lao S. rudnicki by only 1.4% in cyt-b; the two may be one variable species (Vongphayloth et al. 2026).

Sergentomyia iyengari☰
Update from later literature
S. iyengari group: see update note on lewis1978 couplet 122a (S. hivernus, S. khawi). S. iyengari Group: Prasetyo et al. (2026) split Cambodian material into two groups (about 14 vs about 17 teeth; spermathecal collar absent vs deep); see lewis1978 122a. S. dvoraki (Laos, 2024) also has 2-12 anterior teeth; separate it by narrow wrinkled spermathecae with long individual ducts (Vongphayloth et al. 2024). S. iyengari group in Laos: S. hivernus, S. dvoraki, S. khawi and a S. gemmea-like form (Vongphayloth et al. 2026). S. dvoraki is not in this key. S. iyengari: 'erect teeth absent or variably to 2 rows' covers S. iyengari (no denticles) and S. hivernus (3 to about 10 denticles in one row, 10-16 teeth, tubular spermathecae), reinstated by Phumee et al. (2017).
20′– Cibarium with 20, equal-sized teeth; erect teeth in 2 rows of 20 in each row and a third, incomplete row with 4 teeth on either side; pigment patch beet-shaped and anterior projection not reaching arch ⚠
