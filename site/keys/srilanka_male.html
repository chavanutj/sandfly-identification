Genus Phlebotomus→ P1
Update from later literature
The Sri Lanka keys do not include Idiophlebotomus, first recorded from Sri Lanka by Kumari et al. 2026 (Gampaha and Galle districts; 5 males). Its style has only 3 spines, neither terminal nor subterminal, and there is a pair of intra-abdominal rods; it fits neither G1a nor G1b. Use the Lewis 1982 Idiophlebotomus key.
G1′– Style with 4-5 spines, usually terminal; if not all spines terminal, 2 spines terminal and 2 sub-terminal, often in pairs (Fig. 1g) ⚠

Update from later literature
The Sri Lanka keys do not include Idiophlebotomus, first recorded from Sri Lanka by Kumari et al. 2026 (Gampaha and Galle districts; 5 males). Its style has only 3 spines, neither terminal nor subterminal, and there is a pair of intra-abdominal rods; it fits neither G1a nor G1b. Use the Lewis 1982 Idiophlebotomus key. S. nayanasis (Sri Lanka, Anuradhapura; Kumari et al. 2026) is not in this key. ♂: style with two terminal and two subterminal spines; aedeagus thick, finger-shaped; paramere curved downward with rounded end and a ventral hairy tubercle; outer hairs of coxite evenly spaced; cibarium with 10-12 teeth in one line. Via S1a it runs to S2a (S. dentata) or S3b (S. punjabensis); the subgeneric characters would also fit S8a (S. malayae).

Sergentomyia dentata☰
Update from later literature
Compare S. phadangensis ♂ (Thailand; Polseela et al. 2016): style with four terminal spines but cibarial teeth not uniform (3-5 large teeth on each side plus 12-20 denticles), so it fits neither lead of couplet S2 fully. S. dentata: a male S. nayanasis (Kumari et al. 2026; Neophlebotomus) also has a thick finger-shaped aedeagus and two terminal plus two subterminal style spines; cibarium with 10-12 teeth in one line. See note on G1b.
