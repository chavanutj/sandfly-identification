Euphlebotomus species (argentipes group)

Key to Euphlebotomus, the subgenus of the kala-azar vector P. argentipes. Species described later (P. barguesae, P. mascomai, P. seowpohi) are not included.

SourceLewis, D.J. (1982) A taxonomic review of the genus Phlebotomus (Diptera: Psychodidae). Bulletin of the British Museum (Natural History), Entomology 45(2): 121-209.

Phlebotomus kiangsuensis☰
Update from later literature
P. kiangsuensis: see update note on lewis1978 couplet 32a (Prasetyo et al. 2026). Compare P. barguesae ♀ (Thai caves): spermathecae smooth, without striations, head not individualised (Depaquit et al. 2009).
F4 Spermathecal common duct with rather thin walls. Antenna 5 without papilla ⚠
Phlebotomus argentipes☰
Update from later literature
Compare P. mascomai ♀ (Thai caves; Muller et al. 2007): spermatheca with 20-30 rings (12-15 in P. argentipes); ascoid formula 2/III-XIV, 1/XV (P. argentipes 2/III-X, 1/XI-XV). P. argentipes in SE Asia: no P. argentipes was found in Laos 2014-2024; argentipes-like specimens were P. seowpohi, and SE Asian records of P. argentipes need re-investigation (Vongphayloth et al. 2026).
F4′– Spermathecal common duct with thick walls. Antenna 5 with papilla

Update from later literature
Galati et al. 2025 (p. 24) list this taxon as the species P. (Euphlebotomus) gouldi Lewis, 1978 (Thailand). Compare P. mascomai ♀ (Thai caves): antenna 3/epipharynx 1.15-1.30, spermatheca with 20-30 rings (Muller et al. 2007).

Phlebotomus argentipes☰
Update from later literature
Compare P. mascomai ♂ (paramere without lateral spine, superior clasper long and narrow; Muller et al. 2007) and P. barguesae ♂ (aedeagus short, thick, brown with blunt tip; Depaquit et al. 2009), both from Thai caves. P. argentipes in SE Asia: no P. argentipes was found in Laos 2014-2024; argentipes-like specimens were P. seowpohi, and SE Asian records of P. argentipes need re-investigation (Vongphayloth et al. 2026).
M4′– Main lobe of paramere about twice length of middle lobe
→ M5
M5 Aedeagus without accessory spines ⚠
Phlebotomus tumenensis☰
Update from later literature
Males of P. mascomai and P. barguesae (Thai caves) also lack a spine along the aedeagus and may reach this lead: P. mascomai has coxite with 12-15 medio-distal setae and genital filaments 4.3-4.6 times pump (Muller et al. 2007); P. barguesae has a short thick brown aedeagus with blunt tip and coxite without tuft (Depaquit et al. 2009).
M5′– Aedeagus with accessory spines. Lower lobe of paramere appearing narrow but extending mesally, depth of paramere about 0.35 of its length

Phlebotomus philippinensis gouldi☰
Update from later literature
