How it keys out

The chain of characters leading to this taxon in each key (a ready-made diagnosis).

Idiophlebotomus species (Lewis 1982) – couplet F6b
  1. 0a Female
  2. F1a Cibarial armature with median rod or big tooth
  3. F2b Cibarial median rod with minute serrations or none
  4. F3b Cibarium with patch of teeth
  5. F4b Median cibarial projection long and rod-shaped
  6. F5b Cibarial teeth not in radiating lines
  7. F6b Cibarial teeth not very long and parallel
Idiophlebotomus species (Lewis 1982) – couplet M5a
  1. 0b Male
  2. M1b Apical spine of style without such expansion
  3. M2b Style with more than three spines
  4. M4b Style with five spines
  5. M5a Aedeagus prominent and capitate. Paramere slender and without dorsal appendage
Oriental Phlebotominae: species and subspecies (Lewis 1978) – couplet 7a
  1. 1a Cibarial teeth absent or, if present, usually in the form of spicules and not in a definite row (Fig. 27). Pigment patch nearly always absent. Hind ends of abdominal tergites 2-6 with many erect hairs (Fig. 1), sockets as large as on 1. Style of ♂ with three to five spines (Figs 19, 25) and sometimes (some species of subgenus Idiophlebotomus) with two hairs as well
  2. 2a Cibarium with teeth. Pharynx unarmed. Palp not extending further than antenna 3. Style of ♂ with three to five spines, and a pair of intra-abdominal rods ⚠
  3. 3a Female
  4. 4a Cibarial armature with median rod
  5. 5b Cibarial median rod with minute serrations or none
  6. 6b Cibarium with teeth
  7. 7a Cibarial teeth all short
