Phlebotomus papatasi (Scopoli)

ClassificationGenus Phlebotomus, subgenus Phlebotomus
Statusvalid
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Bangladesh, India, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka (Galati et al. 2025)
Thailandwith P. argentipes reported at low abundance from Saraburi, Satun, Phitsanulok, Phang Nga, Surat Thani, Trang and Phayao; specimens from Trang tested for viruses (Soe et al. 2026, citing Phumee et al. 2021a, b); not in the same paper's 42-species checklist
ImportanceRhabdoviridae and Orbivirus RNA detected in Thai specimens from Trang (Soe et al. 2026, Table 2)
SourcesLewis 1978, Dantas-Torres et al. 2014, Akhoundi et al. 2025, Galati et al. 2025, Soe et al. 2026

Oriental Phlebotominae: species and subspecies (Lewis 1978) – couplet 17a
  1. 1a Cibarial teeth absent or, if present, usually in the form of spicules and not in a definite row (Fig. 27). Pigment patch nearly always absent. Hind ends of abdominal tergites 2-6 with many erect hairs (Fig. 1), sockets as large as on 1. Style of ♂ with three to five spines (Figs 19, 25) and sometimes (some species of subgenus Idiophlebotomus) with two hairs as well ⚠
  2. 2b Cibarium with spicules or unarmed. Pharynx usually armed. Palp extending further than antenna 3
  3. 15a Coxite of ♂ with sub-basal hairy process. Genital filaments 1.3-2.3 times as long as pump
  4. 16a Coxite 0.37-0.74 mm long, its process very small. Style long and cylindrical with three terminal spatulate spines and two other spines. Paramere with two dorsal processes. Surstyle with terminal spines. Pharyngeal armature comprising either a network of lines or scales. Spermatheca with nearly equal segments and a refractive membrane (Figs 2, 3) near bases of ductules
  5. 17a Female ⚠

Italy (comparison key) (Dantas-Torres et al. 2014) – couplet M2b
  1. G1b Cibarial teeth and pigment patch usually absent (Fig. 1C). Hind end of abdominal tergites 2-6 with many erect setae, which arise from large sockets of the same size as those on tergite 1 (Fig. 1D). Male style with 2 long apical spines (Fig. 2B, D-H) or 3 short apical spines (Fig. 2C) and setiform spine absent. Female spermathecae with superficial striation (Fig. 3B) or annulated (Fig. 3C-H)
  2. G2a Male
  3. M1a Coxite (= basistyle) with bristles in the basal region
  4. M2b Style with 5 short spines: 3 apical and 2 external spines in its apical third. Paramere with 2 dorsal ramifications. Surstyle with 2 short distal spines (Fig. 2C)
