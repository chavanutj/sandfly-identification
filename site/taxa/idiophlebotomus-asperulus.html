Idiophlebotomus asperulus Quate & Fairchild, 1961

ClassificationGenus Idiophlebotomus
Statusvalid
Other names in keysPhlebotomus asperulus
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Malaysia, Thailand (Galati et al. 2025)
ThailandThailand (Galati et al. 2025) | Chumphon (as P. asperulus) (Polseela et al. 2024); Kanchanaburi (Phra and Taklor caves) (Soomro et al. 2025); listed in the 42-species Thai checklist (Soe et al. 2026, Table 3) | Surat Thani (Wat Tham Khuha Cave; 72.1% of females, 87.2% of males there) and Nakhon Si Thammarat (Wat Khao Khun Phanom Cave); dominant cave species (270 of 462 cave specimens); recorded in the Satun cave by Thammapalo et al. 2020 but not found there (Klaiklueng et al. 2025, Tables 1-3)
ImportanceTrypanosoma noyesi DNA and An04/Frog1 Trypanosoma lineage DNA in specimens from Chumphon (Srisuton et al. 2019, cited in Klaiklueng et al. 2025)
SourcesLewis 1978, Lewis 1982, Galati et al. 2025, Polseela et al. 2024, Soomro et al. 2025, Soe et al. 2026, Klaiklueng et al. 2025

  1. 0a Female
  2. F1a Cibarial armature with median rod or big tooth
  3. F2a Cibarial median rod with large serrations
Idiophlebotomus species (Lewis 1982) – couplet M1a
  1. 0b Male
  2. M1a Apical spine of style with marked basal expansion
Oriental Phlebotominae: species and subspecies (Lewis 1978) – couplet 5a
  1. 1a Cibarial teeth absent or, if present, usually in the form of spicules and not in a definite row (Fig. 27). Pigment patch nearly always absent. Hind ends of abdominal tergites 2-6 with many erect hairs (Fig. 1), sockets as large as on 1. Style of ♂ with three to five spines (Figs 19, 25) and sometimes (some species of subgenus Idiophlebotomus) with two hairs as well ⚠
  2. 2a Cibarium with teeth. Pharynx unarmed. Palp not extending further than antenna 3. Style of ♂ with three to five spines, and a pair of intra-abdominal rods ⚠
  3. 3a Female
  4. 4a Cibarial armature with median rod
  5. 5a Cibarial median rod with large serrations
