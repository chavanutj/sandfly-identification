Phlebotomus stantoni Newstead, 1914

ClassificationGenus Phlebotomus, subgenus Anaphlebotomus
Statusvalid
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Cambodia, China, India, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Sri Lanka, Thailand, Vietnam (Galati et al. 2025) | India: Kerala, Western Ghats (Shah et al. 2024) | Cambodia, in caves and open environments (Prasetyo et al. 2026) | Southern Vietnam, Ho Chi Minh only (Nguyen et al. 2024) | Laos: karstic limestone areas of Vientiane Province (Vongphayloth et al. 2024) | Laos: all seven provinces sampled (Bokeo, Luangnamtha, Luangphabang, Vientiane Capital, Vientiane province, Xiengkhouang, Xayaboury) (Vongphayloth et al. 2026, Table 2)
ThailandPha Tong cave, Chiang Rai (Renaux Torres et al. 2023) | Thailand (Galati et al. 2025) | Ranong, Chumphon, Uthaithani, Lopburi, Phayao, Sakon Nakhon, Buengkan (as P. stantoni) (Polseela et al. 2024); Kanchanaburi (Phra Cave), Saraburi (Tiger Cave), Chachoengsao; two mitochondrial clades (Soomro et al. 2025); Lampang and Songkhla caves (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024); listed in the 42-species Thai checklist (Soe et al. 2026, Table 3) | Songkhla: 2 males, 5 females (Phumee et al. 2017, Table 1) | Surat Thani, Satun, Narathiwat (10 specimens; caves, urban, rural and livestock sites; identified by DNA barcoding) (Klaiklueng et al. 2025, Tables 1-3)
ImportanceDNA of Trypanosoma sp. and of the Leishmania donovani complex detected in specimens from Thailand (cited in Renaux Torres et al. 2023). | L. martiniquensis DNA (Sriwongpan et al. 2021) and Trypanosoma sp. DNA (Phumee et al. 2017) reported (Soe et al. 2026, Table 2) | One female from Songkhla positive for Trypanosoma sp. DNA (ITS1 360 bp, SSU rDNA 939 bp; GenBank KJ467211, KJ467217); not identical to any known Trypanosoma sequence, closest to T. microti and T. kuseli from rodents (94% identity), possibly a novel species; first detection of Trypanosoma in a sand fly from Thailand (Phumee et al. 2017) | Leishmania DNA and An04/Frog1 Trypanosoma DNA reported from Songkhla specimens (Preativatanyou et al. 2023, cited in Klaiklueng et al. 2025)
Diagnostic notes♂ aedeagal ducts 280-336 µm; ♀ spermathecal common duct 178-261 µm, individual ducts 43-63 µm (Renaux Torres et al. 2023). Compare P. shadenae and P. maynei. | Compare P. ajithii (Shah et al. 2024): ♂ gonostyle 150 µm (77 µm in P. stantoni), paramere two-lobed; ♀ spermatheca tubular with very long coiled individual ducts (585-780 µm) and a short common duct (54 µm). | Cambodian females: cibarium generally with four large, very sharp, translucent teeth; morphologically comparable to specimens from Thailand, Laos, Vietnam and Singapore; all SE Asian specimens form one cyt b clade (intraspecific divergence 0.01) (Prasetyo et al. 2026). | Songkhla CytB sequences 100% identical to Ph. stantoni from Chiang Mai and 99% to specimens from Malaysia and Vietnam (Phumee et al. 2017).

Oriental Phlebotominae: species and subspecies (Lewis 1978) – couplet 27a
  1. 1a Cibarial teeth absent or, if present, usually in the form of spicules and not in a definite row (Fig. 27). Pigment patch nearly always absent. Hind ends of abdominal tergites 2-6 with many erect hairs (Fig. 1), sockets as large as on 1. Style of ♂ with three to five spines (Figs 19, 25) and sometimes (some species of subgenus Idiophlebotomus) with two hairs as well ⚠
  2. 2b Cibarium with spicules or unarmed. Pharynx usually armed. Palp extending further than antenna 3
  3. 15b Coxite of ♂ without such process. Genital filaments 3-11 times as long as pump
  4. 24a Style with four long spines of which one is terminal, one subterminal, and two near the middle. Paramere with two or three lobes, with or without accessory spine. Aedeagus short and conical. Pharynx with a small group of teeth in the middle and behind it some concentric lines. Spermatheca segmented, apical segment not enlarged
  5. 25a Female
  6. 26b Spermatheca spindle-shaped with very narrow cylindrical apical segment, duct short but common duct very long. Ascoids long. Palpal segment 3 with peg sensilla grouped around middle
  7. 27a Pharyngeal armature with antero-median numerous long pointed teeth which blend laterally with ridges. Individual ducts longer than spermathecae ⚠

Indo-Chinese species (Thailand to Viet Nam) (Quate 1962) – couplet 2b
  1. 1a Abdominal hairs erect, sockets on tergites 2 to 6 as large as on 1; palpal segment 4 often only 1/2 length of 3; ♂ dististyle with 2 or 3 spines in center; pharynx armed apically; cibarium at most with weak and irregularly developed teeth ⚠
  2. 2b Male dististyle with 4 spines; ♀ with antennal segment 3 extending to tip of proboscis and cibarium with 2 teeth considerably larger than others ⚠
Sri Lanka: females (Wijerathna & Gunathilaka 2020) – couplet P1a
  1. G1a Wings broader, asymmetrical along the length (Fig. 1f). Cibarium unarmed or with scattered spicules, without a pigment patch (Fig. 1d) ⚠
  2. P1a Head of spermatheca with a distinct neck; spermatheca spindle-shaped (Fig. 2a) ⚠
Sri Lanka: males (Wijerathna & Gunathilaka 2020) – couplet P2a
  1. G1a Style with 4 or 5 spines; not all spines terminal (Fig. 1b) ⚠
  2. P1b Paramere without processes or with short ventral processes; style long or short, with long spines (Fig. 2c, d)
  3. P2a Parameres tri-lobed. Style with 4 spines (Fig. 2d) ⚠
