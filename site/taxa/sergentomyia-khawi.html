Sergentomyia khawi (Raynal)

ClassificationGenus Sergentomyia, subgenus Neophlebotomus
Statusvalid
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Cambodia, China, Laos, Thailand (Galati et al. 2025) | Southern Vietnam: all six provinces surveyed, predominant in the south-eastern provinces (Nguyen et al. 2024) | Laos: karstic limestone areas of Vientiane Province (Vongphayloth et al. 2024) | Laos: Bokeo, Luangphabang, Vientiane Capital, Vientiane province, Xiengkhouang (Vongphayloth et al. 2026, Table 2)
ThailandPha Tong cave, Chiang Rai (Renaux Torres et al. 2023) | Thailand (Galati et al. 2025) | Ranong, Chumphon, Uthaithani, Sakon Nakhon, Buengkan (Polseela et al. 2024); Kanchanaburi (Phra Cave), Saraburi (university campus), Chachoengsao, Phatthalung (Soomro et al. 2025); Chiang Rai and Songkhla caves (dominant in Songkhla) (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024); listed in the 42-species Thai checklist (Soe et al. 2026, Table 3) | Songkhla: 5 males, 5 females; reported as a new country record (Phumee et al. 2017, Table 1) | Surat Thani, Satun, Narathiwat (166 specimens; caves, urban, rural and livestock sites); most abundant species in Satun (62.3% of females) and Narathiwat (78.6% of females); newly detected in the Satun cave (Samnaksong Tham Rakhang Thong) surveyed by Thammapalo et al. 2020; not found in Nakhon Si Thammarat (Klaiklueng et al. 2025, Tables 1-3)
ImportanceTrypanosoma sp. (anuran clade) DNA in 2 and Bartonella DNA in 1 female from Songkhla (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024). Soe et al. 2026 summarise DNA of L. martiniquensis, L. orientalis, Trypanosoma spp., Bartonella and Orbivirus and human blood meals; vector competence unproven | Trypanosoma DNA (18S rRNA) in pools of females from Narathiwat (Tanyong Limo Village, rural; Pikun Thong Royal Development Study Center, urban): 99.83-100% identity (603 bp) and 99.48% (580 bp) with Trypanosoma sp. previously found in S. khawi from Chanthaburi and Songkhla; An04/Frog1 lineage of the anuran clade. No Leishmania DNA in the females screened (Klaiklueng et al. 2025)
Diagnostic notesThai specimens: 13-17 cibarial teeth with many vertical teeth in 2-3 rows (Renaux Torres et al. 2023). No ascoidal spurs. | Galati et al. 2025 (p. 31) list it as Sergentomyia (Rondanomyia) khawi Raynal, 1936. Galati et al. 2025 (p. 38) treat S. khawi as one of the populations of S. iyengari s.l. later considered species; no arguments yet to decide their status. | Original description (Raynal 1936, summarised in Prasetyo et al. 2026, Table 7): sclerotised area like a flattened German spiked helmet pointing forward; tooth row sinuous; about 20 fairly sharp teeth 8-9 µm long; 2-3 rows of vertical teeth; spermathecae smooth cylindrical bags 3 times as long as wide (60 x 20 µm), individual ducts 10 µm wide. Type material not found (Prasetyo et al. 2026). Vietnamese COI/cytb sequences matched both Se. khawi and Se. gemmea references from Thailand (Nguyen et al. 2024). Se. khawi and Se. iyengari are molecularly distinct (Chung et al. 2025). | Thai females: 15-20 cibarial teeth and numerous vertical teeth generally in two rows (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024) | Compare Se. dvoraki: Se. khawi has smooth spermathecae with wide individual ducts and many anterior teeth in 2-3 rows (Vongphayloth et al. 2024). | Compare S. nayanasis (Sri Lanka; Kumari et al. 2026): similar spermathecae, but ♀ cibarium with 8-10 robust, darkly pigmented teeth in a single transverse row and spermatheca smooth with a relatively wide individual duct and minute apical projections; COI about 5%. Kumari et al. (p. 280, citing Vongphayloth et al. 2024) describe S. khawi ♀ with 10-12 hind teeth, two or three rows of fore teeth and a lightly developed pigment patch; spermatheca narrow, slightly wrinkled, knob in a deep narrow pit. | Southern Thai specimens: sequences highly similar to both S. khawi and S. gemmea in GenBank; none showed ascoids with spurs (present in S. gemmea, absent in S. khawi), so identified as S. khawi. ASAP (cox1) partitions for S. bailyi, S. khawi and Sergentomyia sp. did not match the other analyses (Klaiklueng et al. 2025).
SourcesLewis 1978, Quate 1962, Depaquit et al. 2019, Renaux Torres et al. 2023, Galati et al. 2025, Prasetyo et al. 2026, Nguyen et al. 2024, Chung et al. 2025, Polseela et al. 2024, Soomro et al. 2025, Sunantaraporn et al. 2024, Soe et al. 2026, Vongphayloth et al. 2024, Vongphayloth et al. 2026, Kumari et al. 2026, Phumee et al. 2017, Klaiklueng et al. 2025
