Sergentomyia barraudi (Sinton)

ClassificationGenus Sergentomyia, subgenus Parrotomyia
Statusvalid
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Bangladesh, Cambodia, China, Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, Laos, Myanmar, Malaysia, Taiwan, Thailand, Vietnam (Galati et al. 2025) | Cambodia, as Se. barraudi Group (Prasetyo et al. 2026) | Southern Vietnam: all six provinces and all habitats (Nguyen et al. 2024) | Taiwan, as Se. barraudi s.l. Diagnostic notesS. barraudi s.s. (India) about 40 cibarial teeth. Thai material with 44-54 teeth referred to S. siamensis; S. maiae has 57-69 teeth (Renaux Torres et al. 2023). | Galati et al. 2025 (p. 38): S. barraudi most likely a species complex; taxonomy and distribution questionable, to be used with caution. Printed subgenus '(Parromyia)' (p. 29), evidently Parrotomyia. | Se. barraudi Group (Prasetyo et al. 2026): Sinton's original female has about 40 palisade-like teeth and a mushroom-shaped pigmented area with a stem divided in two. Cambodian females fall into two groups (tooth-count peaks at 35 and 46): Group 1 fewer than 42 teeth (= Se. barraudi s.s.), Group 2 more than 44 teeth (related to Se. siamensis but molecularly different from Thai/Lao Se. siamensis); both with anterior bifurcation of the sclerotised area. Se. barraudi var. kwangsiensis and var. siulamensis (about 70 teeth) are considered absent from SE Asia. Six cyt b populations in the group; use 'Se. barraudi Group'. Taiwan (Chung et al. 2025): bell-shaped sclerotized area with distinctly separated ends, 40-48 small posterior teeth, conspicuous pharyngeal teeth; a third lineage (COI divergence 0.1686-0.2354 from the other two). Southern Vietnam: high intraspecific divergence (COI 0.098-0.100; cytb 0.068-0.078), possible cryptic species (Nguyen et al. 2024). | Type females (India): smooth capsulated spermathecae, forked sclerotized area and 40 palisade cibarial teeth in a straight line (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024) | Songkhla specimens formed two highly supported CytB clusters without apparent morphological differences; the authors use Se. barraudi s.l. as it could be a species complex (Phumee et al. 2017). | Southern Thai specimens resembled S. siamensis, but cytb showed 98.9% identity with S. barraudi (MK442500) and 91.23% with S. siamensis (PQ151928); cox1 sequences fell into two ASAP groups (Klaiklueng et al. 2025).
