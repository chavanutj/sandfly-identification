Species › Sergentomyia › Neophlebotomus

Sergentomyia ashwanii Saini et al., 2024

ClassificationGenus Sergentomyia, subgenus Neophlebotomus
Statusvalid
DistributionIndia: Kerala (Western Ghats). Thrissur district: Kadaar tribal settlement of Sasthampoovam (Panchayat Mattathoor, Taluk Mukundapuram), 10°22'05.5"N 76°26'32.8"E, altitude 262 feet; Kollam district: Kani tribal settlement (Panchayat Kulathupuzha, Taluk Pathanapuram), 8°56'23.5"N 77°1'48.1"E. Survey January-December 2022; 5% of the total species composition; mainly collected in indoor human dwellings. Type locality not stated explicitly: paratypes from Sasthampoovam (Thrissur); holotype female and allotype male 'collected from the same habitat'. Holotype and allotype in ICMR-Vector Control Research Centre, Puducherry; paratypes in the Zoological Survey of India, Kolkata (Saini et al. 2024).
ImportanceMainly collected in indoor human dwellings; infection status not assessed (Saini et al. 2024).
Diagnostic notesMeasurements in mm (Table 1, n=10 per sex; means unless a range is given). ♀: body 1.86; head 0.35 x 0.40; labrum 0.15-0.17 (printed 'Labarum'); maxilla with 17 ventral and 7 lateral teeth; palpal formula 5,4,3,2,1 (P1-P5 0.08, 0.11, 0.13, 0.14, 0.31); first three measured antennal segments 0.23, 0.10, 0.10 (Roman numerals printed in a non-standard font; the female text gives the formula, as rendered, 'A Ⅲ > A Ⅰ + A Ⅱ', which contradicts these values; male text 'A Ⅰ > A Ⅱ + A Ⅲ'); ascoid 0.03; cibarium with 10-12 hind teeth in a single row, tapering to fine points, and 4-6 small denticles (fore teeth) in a row adjacent to them; pigment patch light golden brown, funnel-shaped; no dorsal process; pharynx nearly slender, 0.14 x 0.05, 'unarmed with minute spicules' (as printed), armature depth 0.02; wing 1.54 x 0.41; spermatheca a thin-walled capsule with cross-striations, 0.58 x 0.24 (as printed, mm), with secretory cells at the distal end; individual duct narrow, length not clear; common duct not evidently visible; cerci 0.12. ♂: body 1.71; labrum 0.14; cibarium with faint 10-12 parallel but irregular hind teeth, denticles present but indistinct, funnel-shaped pigment patch less distinct than in the female; pharynx 0.13 x 0.04, narrowing anteriorly; wing 1.40 x 0.27; genital pump 0.10, genital filaments 0.30 (striated), ratio 3.02; aedeagus slender with blunt tip; paramere hooked; coxite 0.19; style 0.09 with two terminal and two subterminal spines 0.01 apart and an accessory spine below the subterminal spine. Compared species (values as given by Saini et al. 2024, citing Lewis 1978 and Srinivasan et al. 2014): S. malabarica (♀ about eight hind teeth in a nearly straight row, distinct pear-shaped pigment patch, pharynx almost unarmed; ♂ ten strewn hind teeth, no fore teeth), S. gemmea (♀ ten hind teeth with broad bases, one row of eight very large fore teeth, two rows of small teeth in front and a patch of small fore teeth on each side, pigment patch pale; ♂ about six irregular hind teeth and about 20 irregular denticles, pigment patch indefinite) and S. monticola (♀ ten teeth in a single row, 3-4 denticles, golden-brown dome-shaped pigment patch; ♂ ten parallel but irregular hind teeth, indistinct fore teeth). None of these has the light golden row of 4-6 denticles. COI: genetic distance 14.2% to congeners (14% in the Variability section); the tree (Fig. 3) includes only S. gemmea, S. monticola and P. argentipes. Inconsistencies as printed: male interocular distance 0.33 in the text but 0.16 in Table 1; female T1 0.36 in the text, mean 0.33 in Table 1. Prasetyo et al. 2026 (p. 9) note that it was described very close to the type locality of S. iyengari s.s. without discussion of its close resemblance or possible synonymy, and include it in the S. iyengari group (Fig. 6); they cite the authority as 'Saini, Shah, Jessu, Anns & Amju, 2024'. Not in any printed key: in Lewis 1978 a ♀ (one row of about ten hind teeth, fewer than three rows of fore teeth) may run to 132a/133b (S. jefferyi / S. malabarica); a ♂ to 150a/150b or 154a.
SourcesSaini et al. 2024, Prasetyo et al. 2026
