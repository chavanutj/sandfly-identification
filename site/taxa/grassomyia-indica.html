Grassomyia indica (Theodor)

ClassificationGenus Grassomyia
Statusvalid
Other names in keysSergentomyia indica
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Cambodia, China, India, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, Taiwan, Thailand (Galati et al. 2025) | Cambodia, as Grassomyia sp., most abundant taxon (21.9%) (Prasetyo et al. 2026) | Southern Vietnam: 1 female (Nguyen et al. 2024) | Laos (Vientiane, Luangphabang; Quate 1962 as squamipleuris; Vongphayloth et al. 2024 as Gr. indica s.l.) | Laos: Bokeo, Luangphabang, Vientiane province, Xayaboury (Vongphayloth et al. 2026, Table 2) | Sri Lanka: specimens referred to the University of Kelaniya in 2024 from Gampaha, Galle and Anuradhapura districts (districts per species not given), n=1 (1.7%) of 58 examined (Kumari et al. 2026), as S. indica
ThailandChiang Mai, Loei, Nong Khai, as 'squamipleuris' (Quate 1962); Grassomyia sp. at Pha Tong (Renaux Torres et al. 2023) | Thailand (Galati et al. 2025) | Uthaithani, Lopburi (as S. indica) (Polseela et al. 2024); Chachoengsao (buffalo farm) (as Gr. indica) (Soomro et al. 2025); Tham Phra, Chiang Rai (as Gr. indica) (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024); listed in the 42-species Thai checklist (Soe et al. 2026, Table 3) (as Sergentomyia indica and as Grassomyia indica) | Songkhla: 4 males, 3 females (as Se. indica) (Phumee et al. 2017, Table 1) | Narathiwat (rural site), 3 females (as G. indica; 'S. indica' in the text) (Klaiklueng et al. 2025, Tables 1-2)
ImportanceLeishmania DNA (Preativatanyou et al. 2023) and Trypanosoma DNA (Srisuton et al. 2019; An04/Frog1 lineage in Phatthalung and Songkhla specimens) reported from Thai specimens (cited in Klaiklueng et al. 2025)
Diagnostic notes♀ convex cibarial tooth row and round, finely spiculate spermatheca; ♂ genital filaments with dilated tips (Lewis 1978). Dark part of pigment belt straight anteriorly (Lewis 1987). | Genus: no ascoids on f1, f1 usually very short, wings usually narrow, spermathecae rounded and echinulate like poppy capsules; species identification often needs thoracic characters (Prasetyo et al. 2026). Original description 33-36 cibarial teeth (Theodor 1931); 78 Cambodian specimens had 19-50 (mean 31.2) but are molecularly homogeneous with G. indica elsewhere; called Grassomyia sp. pending revision (Prasetyo et al. 2026). Taiwan 'Se. squamipleuris' clusters with Se. indica (COI 0.0517, cytb 0.0352); Chung et al. (2025) consider them probably conspecific. | Laos specimens treated as Gr. indica s.l.: SE Asian material probably a species other than the Indian one; 25-33 cibarial teeth (Vongphayloth et al. 2024).
SourcesLewis 1978, Lewis 1987, Quate 1962, Wijerathna & Gunathilaka 2020, Galati et al. 2025, Prasetyo et al. 2026, Nguyen et al. 2024, Chung et al. 2025, Polseela et al. 2024, Soomro et al. 2025, Sunantaraporn et al. 2024, Soe et al. 2026, Vongphayloth et al. 2024, Vongphayloth et al. 2026, Kumari et al. 2026, Phumee et al. 2017, Klaiklueng et al. 2025

Oriental Phlebotominae: species and subspecies (Lewis 1978) – couplet 63a
  1. 1b Cibarial teeth in a transverse row (Fig. 65), fore teeth sometimes present (Figs 136, 245) and usually pointing upward. Pigment patch (Figs 66, 136) usually present. Hind ends of abdominal tergites 2-6 usually with all or most hairs recumbent, most sockets much smaller than on 1 except in S. musai. Style of ♂ with four major spines and an accessory seta (Fig. 80) ⚠
  2. 48b Abdominal tergites 2-6 with few or no erect hairs
  3. 49b Abdominal tergites 2-6 usually with no erect hairs. Spermatheca not segmented but sometimes striated or wrinkled
  4. 63a Spermatheca a round finely spiculate capsule. Cibarium of ♀ with convex comb of pointed teeth. Pharynx of ♀ bulging near hind end. Antenna of ♀ with one ascoid on segments 4-15. Abdominal tergites 2-6 with some posterior erect hairs. Genital filaments with rounded expanded tips. Parameres blunt ⚠
