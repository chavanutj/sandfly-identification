Sergentomyia anodontis (Quate and Faichild, 1961)

ClassificationGenus Sergentomyia, ungrouped
Statusvalid
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Cambodia, Malaysia, Thailand (Galati et al. 2025) | Cambodia: Kampong Trach Mountain and Angkor Chey, Kampot Province (Prasetyo et al. 2026) | Laos, first record as Se. anodontis s.l. (Vongphayloth et al. 2024) | Laos: Luangnamtha, Luangphabang, Vientiane province, Xiengkhouang (karstic areas) (Vongphayloth et al. 2026, Table 2)
ThailandPha Tong cave, Chiang Rai (Renaux Torres et al. 2023) - most abundant species there | Thailand (Galati et al. 2025) | Ranong, Chumphon, Uthaithani, Phayao, Chiang Rai, Sakon Nakhon; most abundant species, 25.7% of females (Polseela et al. 2024); Kanchanaburi (Phra, Taklor and Manow Phee caves), Saraburi (Chulalongkorn University campus) (Soomro et al. 2025); Tham Pha Thai, Lampang; Tham Phra, Chiang Rai; Tham Khao Rup Chang, Songkhla (most abundant in the two northern caves) (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024); listed in the 42-species Thai checklist (Soe et al. 2026, Table 3) | Surat Thani and Satun caves (14 specimens) (Klaiklueng et al. 2025, Tables 1-3)
ImportanceTrypanosoma DNA (T. noyesi and Trypanosoma sp.) in 2 females and Bartonella sp. DNA (close to bat Bartonella) in 12 females from Chiang Rai, Bartonella in 2 from Lampang (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024) | Trypanosoma noyesi DNA in specimens from Chumphon (Srisuton et al. 2019, cited in Klaiklueng et al. 2025)
Diagnostic notesFemale: pharynx thin and unarmed; cibarium unarmed with a median inverted-V projection; spermathecae long tubules not separated from the duct, apical thick hairy knob invaginated, several distal rings. Cambodian specimens also show a structure covering the median projection and small translucent lateral teeth; cyt b variability (0.030-0.045 between populations) suggests a species complex (Prasetyo et al. 2026). | COI separates northern and southern Thai populations (bootstrap 99%), possibly two species; some females have more or less developed lateral vertical teeth (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024) | Laos specimens: paired ascoids on f1-f13 (original description: paired on f1-f11, single on f12-f14); Thai COI data show two clusters; probably a species complex (Vongphayloth et al. 2024). | S. anodontis s.l.: probably a complex of species (Vongphayloth et al. 2026).
SourcesLewis 1978, Renaux Torres et al. 2023, Galati et al. 2025, Prasetyo et al. 2026, Polseela et al. 2024, Soomro et al. 2025, Sunantaraporn et al. 2024, Soe et al. 2026, Vongphayloth et al. 2024, Vongphayloth et al. 2026, Klaiklueng et al. 2025
