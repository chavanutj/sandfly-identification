Sergentomyia nayanasis Kumari et al., 2026

ClassificationGenus Sergentomyia, subgenus Neophlebotomus
Statusvalid
DistributionSri Lanka: Anuradhapura district (North Central Province), 8.007 319°N, 80.517 389°E (as printed); dry zone, scrub-dominated vegetation; peri-domestic environments adjacent to fragmented forest, CDC light traps. Holotype female and paratype male (the only two specimens), received in 2024; deposited in the Department of Parasitology, Faculty of Medicine, University of Kelaniya, Sri Lanka. GenBank COI PX127887 (female), PX226518 (male) (Kumari et al. 2026).
ImportanceCaptured peri-domestically in Anuradhapura, the district with the highest reported cutaneous leishmaniasis burden in Sri Lanka; no Leishmania screening reported. The authors call for vector competence studies (Kumari et al. 2026).
Diagnostic notesName verified in the PDF: 'nayanasis' (47 occurrences, no variant spelling); named after Prof. Nayana Gunathilaka. No authority printed with the name; author given here as the authors of the paper. Placed in Neophlebotomus by recumbent hairs on tergites 2-6, cibarial teeth in a transverse row with distinct pigment patch and upwardly directed fore teeth, A3 longer than A4 + A5, slender weakly armed pharynx, ♂ paramere hooked with ventral hairy tubercle and style with four spines plus accessory seta (Kumari et al. 2026, p. 276). Diagnosis (p. 276): cibarial armature of 8-12 robust, darkly pigmented teeth in a single transverse row; pharynx with fine longitudinal striations, no heavily sclerotized spines. ♀: 8-10 pointed, evenly spaced teeth forming a comb along the posterior margin; pharyngeal armature of fine, evenly spaced longitudinal ridges and striations converging posteriorly; spermatheca smooth, with a relatively wide individual duct and minute projections at the apical end (the diagnosis also lists 'cross striations'); Table 1 records the female spermatheca as 'not visible' (degraded); head 275.43 x 308.70 µm, all other female measurements 'Damaged'. ♂: cibarium with 10-12 teeth in one line; pharynx lightly armed with scales; style with two terminal and two subterminal spines; outer hairs of coxite evenly spaced; paramere curved downward with rounded end; aedeagus thick, finger-shaped (the subgeneric paragraph says 'slender with a blunt tip'); genital filaments transversely striated. Measurements (µm, n=1): head 228.95 x 232.59; antenna (Table 1 rows 'A1', 'A2', 'A3') 219.46, 103.91, 84.58; P1-P5 32.27, 83.49, 106.35, 115.98, 155.52; wing 1218.61 x 302.54; alpha 176.56, beta 284.89, gamma 253.88, delta 51.99; style 95.92; coxite 169.95 x 64.69; individual surstyle 151.25; paramere 141.91; aedeagus 61.73; genital filaments + pump 304.90; genital pump 194.82. Compared species (values as given by Kumari et al. 2026, pp. 280-281): S. khawi ♀ (similar spermathecae; 10-12 hind teeth with two or three rows of fore teeth, lightly developed pigment patch; spermatheca narrow, slightly wrinkled, knob in a deep narrow pit; citing Vongphayloth et al. 2024) and S. iyengari ♂ (14-17 hind teeth, fore teeth absent or in one or two rows, variable pigment patch; citing Cates & Lien 1970). COI K2P distance about 5% to the closest congeners S. khawi and S. iyengari; <1% between holotype and paratype. Doubts (as printed): the results say the two sequences did not form a distinct, strongly supported group, the discussion says they form a monophyletic lineage with 98% bootstrap support; the specimens are said to come from the same location, and also from similar habitats on different dates. Not in any printed key: see update notes on srilanka_female G1b and srilanka_male G1b; in the Sri Lanka male key the thick finger-shaped aedeagus leads to subgenus Sergentomyia (S1a).
