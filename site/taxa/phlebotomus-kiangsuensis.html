Phlebotomus kiangsuensis Yao & Wu, 1938

ClassificationGenus Phlebotomus, subgenus Euphlebotomus
Statusvalid
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Cambodia, China, Laos, Malaysia, Taiwan, Thailand, Vietnam (Galati et al. 2025) | Cambodia, only in caves or cave entrances (Kampot Province), as a provisional 'Ph. kiangsuensis-like' population (Prasetyo et al. 2026) | Taiwan: one specimen, Taitung (Chung et al. 2025) | Bhutan: widespread, 46.2% of Phlebotomus (Wangdi et al. 2024) | Laos, first record (Vongphayloth et al. 2024) | Laos: Luangnamtha, Luangphabang, Vientiane province, Xiengkhouang (Vongphayloth et al. 2026, Table 2)
ThailandThailand (Galati et al. 2025) | Chumphon (Polseela et al. 2024); listed in the 42-species Thai checklist (Soe et al. 2026, Table 3) | Nakhon Si Thammarat (Wat Khao Khun Phanom Cave) only: 6 females, 12 males, identified by DNA barcoding (Klaiklueng et al. 2025, Tables 1-3)
ImportanceConsidered a suspected Leishmania vector in Taiwan because of its tendency to feed on humans (cited in Chung et al. 2025); the single 2017-2018 Taiwan specimen was negative for blood meal and Leishmania DNA (Chung et al. 2025). | Bites humans and attacks bats (Lewis 1978, as cited in Vongphayloth et al. 2024). | Found only in Nakhon Si Thammarat, where a leishmaniasis case was reported and S. khawi was absent; the authors suggest examining its potential role as a leishmaniasis vector (Klaiklueng et al. 2025)
Diagnostic notesGalati et al. 2025 (p. 38): validity of the Chinese P. kiangsuensis questionable; Southeast Asian records likely refer to another, undescribed species. | Cambodian population (Prasetyo et al. 2026): spermathecae striated (original drawing by Yao & Wu 1938 rather smooth), with a distal ring shaped like a brick placed on the body of the spermatheca; pharynx more armed and wider than in the Chinese type material; cyt b divergence 0.158 from Thai/Lao Ph. kiangsuensis; species status withheld pending type material. Taiwan: COI intraspecific divergence 0.1397 (Chung et al. 2025). | First cox1 barcodes for P. kiangsuensis (no GenBank cox1 reference); cytb sequences clustered with reference P. kiangsuensis sequences (Klaiklueng et al. 2025).

Euphlebotomus species (argentipes group) (Lewis 1982) – couplet F3b
  1. 0a Female
  2. F1b Spermatheca not bulbous, without narrow neck. Pharyngeal armature comprising distinct teeth or ridges
  3. F2a Spermatheca with faint transverse striations or indistinct segments
  4. F3b Spermatheca with small apical segment and about 30 transverse striations. Pharynx with a median group of small teeth and, behind them, some close-set concentric lines ⚠
