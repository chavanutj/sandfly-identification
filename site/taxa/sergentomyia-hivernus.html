Sergentomyia hivernus Raynal & Gaschen

ClassificationGenus Sergentomyia, ungrouped
StatusReinstated (Phumee et al. 2016, cited in Depaquit et al. 2019); formerly in S. iyengari
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Laos, Thailand, Vietnam (Galati et al. 2025, as S. hiverna) | Cambodia: Kampong Trach Mountain, Kep National Park, Andong Cros (Kampong Cham) (Prasetyo et al. 2026) | Laos: karstic limestone areas of Vientiane Province (Vongphayloth et al. 2024) | Laos: Bokeo, Luangphabang, Vientiane Capital, Vientiane province, Xayaboury (Vongphayloth et al. 2026, Table 2)
ThailandPha Tong cave, Chiang Rai (Renaux Torres et al. 2023) | Thailand (Galati et al. 2025, as S. hiverna) | Kanchanaburi (Ma Glue Cave) (Soomro et al. 2025); Chiang Rai and Songkhla caves (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024); listed in the 42-species Thai checklist (Soe et al. 2026, Table 3)
Diagnostic notesTubular spermathecae and few vertical cibarial teeth. | Galati et al. 2025 (p. 36) list it as Sergentomyia hiverna (Raynal and Gaschen, 1935) (Sergentomyia incertae sedis). Galati et al. 2025 (p. 38) treat S. hivernus as one of the populations of S. iyengari s.l. later considered species; no arguments yet to decide their status. | Female cibarium and pharynx close to Se. iyengari s.l.; spermathecae smooth and broad, ducts of equal diameter throughout, joining a common canal; head of the spermatheca invaginated within a collar (Prasetyo et al. 2026). Chung et al. (2025) found low COI divergence (0.0416) from non-Taiwan Se. iyengari and suggest they may be one species. | Spermathecae tubular without limit between body and duct, separating it from Se. dvoraki (similar 2-12 anterior teeth). Earlier Laos records of Se. iyengari may refer to Se. hivernus (Vongphayloth et al. 2024).
SourcesDepaquit et al. 2019, Renaux Torres et al. 2023, Galati et al. 2025, Prasetyo et al. 2026, Chung et al. 2025, Soomro et al. 2025, Sunantaraporn et al. 2024, Soe et al. 2026, Vongphayloth et al. 2024, Vongphayloth et al. 2026
