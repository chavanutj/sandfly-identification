Sergentomyia hivernus Raynal & Gaschen, 1935

ClassificationGenus Sergentomyia, ungrouped
StatusReinstated (Phumee et al. 2017, cited in Depaquit et al. 2019); formerly in S. iyengari
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Laos, Thailand, Vietnam (Galati et al. 2025, as S. hiverna) | Cambodia: Kampong Trach Mountain, Kep National Park, Andong Cros (Kampong Cham) (Prasetyo et al. 2026) | Laos: karstic limestone areas of Vientiane Province (Vongphayloth et al. 2024) | Laos: Bokeo, Luangphabang, Vientiane Capital, Vientiane province, Xayaboury (Vongphayloth et al. 2026, Table 2)
ThailandPha Tong cave, Chiang Rai (Renaux Torres et al. 2023) | Thailand (Galati et al. 2025, as S. hiverna) | Kanchanaburi (Ma Glue Cave) (Soomro et al. 2025); Chiang Rai and Songkhla caves (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024); listed in the 42-species Thai checklist (Soe et al. 2026, Table 3) | Songkhla (traps around a leishmaniasis patient's home, September 2013): 3 males, 4 females; reported as a new country record (Phumee et al. 2017, Table 1 p. 430, p. 432)
Diagnostic notesTubular spermathecae and few vertical cibarial teeth. | Galati et al. 2025 (p. 36) list it as Sergentomyia hiverna (Raynal and Gaschen, 1935) (Sergentomyia incertae sedis). Galati et al. 2025 (p. 38) treat S. hivernus as one of the populations of S. iyengari s.l. later considered species; no arguments yet to decide their status. | Female cibarium and pharynx close to Se. iyengari s.l.; spermathecae smooth and broad, ducts of equal diameter throughout, joining a common canal; head of the spermatheca invaginated within a collar (Prasetyo et al. 2026). Chung et al. (2025) found low COI divergence (0.0416) from non-Taiwan Se. iyengari and suggest they may be one species. | Spermathecae tubular without limit between body and duct, separating it from Se. dvoraki (similar 2-12 anterior teeth). Earlier Laos records of Se. iyengari may refer to Se. hivernus (Vongphayloth et al. 2024). | Reinstated by Phumee et al. 2017 (p. 433; J. Med. Entomol. 54: 429-434, Advance Access 2016), rejecting Quate's (1962) synonymy with Se. iyengari: spermathecae long, wide, smooth and tubular without limits between body and duct (Se. iyengari: shorter, with an easily observed limit between body and duct); cibarium with denticles (~10 in the original description; 3 to 8, mean 5.4, on one row in Songkhla females), whereas Se. iyengari is described without denticles (Sinton 1933); cibarial teeth 10-16 (14 in the original description); the authors consider 10-16 cibarial teeth and 3 to ~10 cibarial denticles the most important characters. Antennal segment III (flagellomere 1) 218-239 µm (mean 230 µm) in Songkhla specimens vs 250-285 µm in Raynal & Gaschen (1935). Described from Vietnam as Ph. hibernus, changed to Ph. hivernus by Raynal (1935) (Phumee et al. 2017, p. 432).
