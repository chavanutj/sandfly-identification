Phlebotomus mascittii

ClassificationGenus Phlebotomus, subgenus Transphlebotomus
Statusvalid
SourcesDantas-Torres et al. 2014

How it keys out

The chain of characters leading to this taxon in each key (a ready-made diagnosis).

Italy (comparison key) (Dantas-Torres et al. 2014) – couplet M3a
  1. G1b Cibarial teeth and pigment patch usually absent (Fig. 1C). Hind end of abdominal tergites 2-6 with many erect setae, which arise from large sockets of the same size as those on tergite 1 (Fig. 1D). Male style with 2 long apical spines (Fig. 2B, D-H) or 3 short apical spines (Fig. 2C) and setiform spine absent. Female spermathecae with superficial striation (Fig. 3B) or annulated (Fig. 3C-H)
  2. G2a Male
  3. M1b Coxite without bristles in the basal region
  4. M3a Aedeagus with the distal region tapered and cup-like expansion at the anterior end of genital pump thin-walled and colourless (Fig. 2D)
Italy (comparison key) (Dantas-Torres et al. 2014) – couplet F1a
  1. G1b Cibarial teeth and pigment patch usually absent (Fig. 1C). Hind end of abdominal tergites 2-6 with many erect setae, which arise from large sockets of the same size as those on tergite 1 (Fig. 1D). Male style with 2 long apical spines (Fig. 2B, D-H) or 3 short apical spines (Fig. 2C) and setiform spine absent. Female spermathecae with superficial striation (Fig. 3B) or annulated (Fig. 3C-H)
  2. G2b Female
  3. F1a Spermathecae not ringed, with transverse striations often in distal part. Pharynx with large irregular teeth (Figs 3B, 4B)
