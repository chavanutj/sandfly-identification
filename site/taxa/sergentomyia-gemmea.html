Sergentomyia gemmea Lewis & Jeffery, 1978

ClassificationGenus Sergentomyia, subgenus Neophlebotomus
Statusvalid
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Laos, Malaysia, Thailand (Galati et al. 2025)
ThailandRare: 1/261 females in Thai samples (Depaquit et al. 2019) | Thailand (Galati et al. 2025) | listed in the 42-species Thai checklist (Soe et al. 2026, Table 3) | Phangnga, Surat Thani, Nakhon Si Thammarat: 81.4% of sand flies (Sukra et al. 2013, cited in Phumee et al. 2017); identity doubtful (see conflicts)
ImportanceRepeatedly suspected as a vector of L. martiniquensis in Thailand; role 'very questionable' (Depaquit et al. 2019). | DNA of L. siamensis/L. orientalis and L. donovani reported in Thai specimens (Soe et al. 2026, citing Chusri et al. 2014 and Sriwongpan et al. 2021); vector competence unproven | Klaiklueng et al. 2025: southern Thai sequences matching S. gemmea came from specimens without spurred ascoids (S. khawi); the reported predominance of S. gemmea and its suspected role in L. martiniquensis transmission there (Preativatanyou et al. 2023) may require re-evaluation. An04/Frog1 Trypanosoma DNA reported in 'S. gemmea' from Songkhla and Phatthalung (Srisuton et al. 2019, cited in Klaiklueng et al. 2025)
Diagnostic notesDecisive character: ascoids with anterior spurs (Depaquit et al. 2019). Many GenBank 'S. gemmea' sequences are probably S. khawi or S. hivernus. | Galati et al. 2025 (p. 31) list it as Sergentomyia (Rondanomyia) gemmea Lewis and Jeffery, 1978. | Laos records pending revision: cibarium appears different and Cyt b distance between Laos and Thai specimens is 0.06 (Vongphayloth et al. 2024). | Laotian records of S. gemmea are pending revision; Vongphayloth et al. (2026) list Lao specimens as 'Se. gemmea-like (Se. sp 1)'. | Compare S. ashwanii (Kerala; Saini et al. 2024): ♀ with 10-12 hind teeth in a single row plus only one row of 4-6 small denticles; COI distance 14.2% to S. gemmea and S. monticola.

Oriental Phlebotominae: species and subspecies (Lewis 1978) – couplet 115a
  1. 1b Cibarial teeth in a transverse row (Fig. 65), fore teeth sometimes present (Figs 136, 245) and usually pointing upward. Pigment patch (Figs 66, 136) usually present. Hind ends of abdominal tergites 2-6 usually with all or most hairs recumbent, most sockets much smaller than on 1 except in S. musai. Style of ♂ with four major spines and an accessory seta (Fig. 80) ⚠
  2. 48b Abdominal tergites 2-6 with few or no erect hairs
  3. 49b Abdominal tergites 2-6 usually with no erect hairs. Spermatheca not segmented but sometimes striated or wrinkled
  4. 63b Spermatheca elongate and usually without spicules
  5. 64b Without this combination. Spermatheca usually not tubular
  6. 69b Without this combination
  7. 110a Cibarial tooth-row usually comprising parallel teeth, often nearly equal but not very narrow. Pharynx slender, with teeth or scales, or nearly unarmed. Antenna 3 long, longer than 4+5, often 1.25-2.00 times length of labrum. Wing rather broad, R2 usually longer than R2+3. Spermatheca often a thin-walled capsule, sometimes with cross-striations; duct sometimes narrow and joining common duct. Aedeagus usually slender with blunt tip. Paramere hooked. Style with two terminal spines and two others more or less terminal or near the middle ⚠
