Phlebotomus betisi Lewis & Wharton, 1963

ClassificationGenus Phlebotomus, subgenus Larroussius
Statusvalid
DistributionRecords in the project regions: Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam (Galati et al. 2025) | Malaysia: type locality a cave in Betis, Gua Musang; males described from Kota Gelanggi cave, Pahang, 20 km from the type locality (Vongphayloth et al. 2023, p. 2)
ThailandThailand (Galati et al. 2025) | Tham Khao Rup Chang, Songkhla (Sunantaraporn et al. 2024); listed in the 42-species Thai checklist (Soe et al. 2026, Table 3) | Samnaksong Tham Rakhang Thong Cave, Satun (Thammapalo et al. 2020, cited in Klaiklueng et al. 2025); not found there in the 2020 samples of Klaiklueng et al. 2025
ImportanceTrypanosoma noyesi DNA and An04/Frog1 Trypanosoma lineage DNA in specimens from Chumphon (Srisuton et al. 2019, cited in Klaiklueng et al. 2025)
Diagnostic notesGalati et al. 2025 (p. 25) list it as Phlebotomus (Lewisius) betisi Lewis and Wharton, 1963. Galati et al. 2025 (p. 38): type species of subgenus Lewisius, formerly in Larroussius; records outside Malaysia should be re-evaluated (may refer to P. breyi or another species). | Unidentified Ph. (Lewisius) spp. from Surat Thani and Nakhon Si Thammarat caves (17 specimens) form a cytb clade sister to P. betisi and split into two putative species (ASAP). Lewisius as summarised by Klaiklueng et al. 2025 after Vongphayloth et al. 2023: five prominent spines (two terminal, one subapical external, one apical third external, one median internal), simple paramere and conical paramere sheath in males; females with segmented spermathecae with bead-like rings and a long terminal process enclosing a neck. | Vongphayloth et al. 2023 (p. 7) made Ph. betisi the type species of the new subgenus Lewisius Depaquit & Vongphayloth: pharynx with numerous small point-like teeth as in Larroussius; gonostyle with five long spines, two terminal, the upper external subapical, the lower external in the apical third and the inner one in the middle; simple parameres; conical, regularly tapering parameral sheath; spermathecae of bead-like rings with a long terminal process. Differential diagnosis (Table 6, p. 19) against Ph. breyi and Ph. sinxayarami (Laos): ♀ antennal papillae on f1-f2, f9-f14; f1/labrum > 1; p4 < 141 µm (126-141); spermathecae with about 20 clearly separated rings, long neck, individual ducts < 400 µm, no common duct. ♂ ascoid formula 2/f1-f6, 1/f7-f12, 0/f13-f14 (both new species 2/f1-f9, 1/f10-f11, 0/f12-f14); papillae on f1-f2, f9-f14; aedeagal ducts < 500 µm, aedeagal ducts/sperm pump < 4. Malaysian specimens (Tables 4-5, pp. 17-18; min-max): ♀ (n = 3) p4 126.39-140.66 µm, p5 393.31-452.85 µm, f1/labrum 1.04-1.19; ♂ aedeagal ducts 331-470 µm, AD/sperm pump 2.74-3.92, p4 99-121 µm, f1/(f2+f3) 1.07-1.17. Interocular suture incomplete in both sexes (as in Ph. breyi; complete in Ph. sinxayarami) (p. 16). The authors call for re-checking all Ph. betisi records outside the type area, including Thailand and Vietnam (p. 16).

Oriental Phlebotominae: species and subspecies (Lewis 1978) – couplet 41a
  1. 1a Cibarial teeth absent or, if present, usually in the form of spicules and not in a definite row (Fig. 27). Pigment patch nearly always absent. Hind ends of abdominal tergites 2-6 with many erect hairs (Fig. 1), sockets as large as on 1. Style of ♂ with three to five spines (Figs 19, 25) and sometimes (some species of subgenus Idiophlebotomus) with two hairs as well ⚠
  2. 2b Cibarium with spicules or unarmed. Pharynx usually armed. Palp extending further than antenna 3
  3. 15b Coxite of ♂ without such process. Genital filaments 3-11 times as long as pump
  4. 24b Style with five long spines
  5. 30b Paramere without ventral process. Pharyngeal armature otherwise ⚠
  6. 38b Paramere not truncated
  7. 39a Pharynx of ♀ and ♂ with punctiform teeth. Spermatheca segmented, with long finger-like process. Genital filaments 3-5 times as long as pump ⚠
  8. 40a Female
  9. 41a Pharynx with scarcely visible spicules. Spermatheca with about 22 bead-like segments, a long neck and a very small head. West Malaysia ⚠
