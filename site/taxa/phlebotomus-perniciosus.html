The chain of characters leading to this taxon in each key (a ready-made diagnosis).

Italy (comparison key) (Dantas-Torres et al. 2014) – couplet M5a
  1. G1b Cibarial teeth and pigment patch usually absent (Fig. 1C). Hind end of abdominal tergites 2-6 with many erect setae, which arise from large sockets of the same size as those on tergite 1 (Fig. 1D). Male style with 2 long apical spines (Fig. 2B, D-H) or 3 short apical spines (Fig. 2C) and setiform spine absent. Female spermathecae with superficial striation (Fig. 3B) or annulated (Fig. 3C-H)
  2. G2a Male
  3. M1b Coxite without bristles in the basal region
  4. M3b Aedeagus with the distal region bifid or rounded
  5. M4b Aedeagus without colourless plate on terminal portion
  6. M5a Aedeagus bifid at the end (Fig. 2F)
Italy (comparison key) (Dantas-Torres et al. 2014) – couplet F5a
  1. G1b Cibarial teeth and pigment patch usually absent (Fig. 1C). Hind end of abdominal tergites 2-6 with many erect setae, which arise from large sockets of the same size as those on tergite 1 (Fig. 1D). Male style with 2 long apical spines (Fig. 2B, D-H) or 3 short apical spines (Fig. 2C) and setiform spine absent. Female spermathecae with superficial striation (Fig. 3B) or annulated (Fig. 3C-H)
  2. G2b Female
  3. F1b Spermathecae ringed. Pharynx not as above
  4. F2b Spermathecae with long finger-like neck
  5. F4a Spermathecae with lateral structures at the base of the ducts
  6. F5a Spermathecae with 8-12 rings, with thin neck and small, rounded head. Pharyngeal armature occupying more than a quarter length of pharynx, with teeth arranged disorderly (Figs 3E, 4E)
