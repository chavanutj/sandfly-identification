ClassificationGenus Phlebotomus, subgenus Larroussius
Statusvalid
Diagnostic notesTaxonomic status uncertain according to Akhoundi et al. 2025 (p. 96, footnote to the Larroussius male key); males keyed together with P. transcaucasicus.

How it keys out

The chain of characters leading to this taxon in each key (a ready-made diagnosis).

Italy (comparison key) (Dantas-Torres et al. 2014) – couplet M4a
  1. G1b Cibarial teeth and pigment patch usually absent (Fig. 1C). Hind end of abdominal tergites 2-6 with many erect setae, which arise from large sockets of the same size as those on tergite 1 (Fig. 1D). Male style with 2 long apical spines (Fig. 2B, D-H) or 3 short apical spines (Fig. 2C) and setiform spine absent. Female spermathecae with superficial striation (Fig. 3B) or annulated (Fig. 3C-H)
  2. G2a Male
  3. M1b Coxite without bristles in the basal region
  4. M3b Aedeagus with the distal region bifid or rounded
  5. M4a Aedeagus with colourless plate (transparent process) on terminal portion (Fig. 2E)

  1. G1b Cibarial teeth and pigment patch usually absent (Fig. 1C). Male style with 2 long apical spines (Fig. 2B, D-H) or 3 short apical spines (Fig. 2C) and setiform spine absent. Female spermathecae with superficial striation (Fig. 3B) or annulated (Fig. 3C-H)
  2. G2b Female
  3. F1b Spermathecae ringed. Pharynx not as above
  4. F2b Spermathecae with long finger-like neck
  5. F4a Spermathecae with lateral structures at the base of the ducts
  6. F5b Spermathecae with 12-16 rings, small neck that narrows before the small, oval head. Pharyngeal armature about a quarter (or less) length of pharynx with teeth ending anteriorly in a clear line of demarcation (Figs 3F, 4F)
